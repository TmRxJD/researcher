# Evidence review and reproducibility

Use this workflow when the user needs broad coverage, reproducibility, or a formal evidence synthesis. For rapid work, state which steps were abbreviated and what may have been missed. Use a discipline-specific method when one applies.

## Plan before screening

- State the review question and the decisions it informs. For intervention questions, consider population, intervention, comparator, and outcomes. For mapping a topic or reviewing historical evidence, define the concepts, context, and eligible evidence instead.
- Specify eligibility for dates, languages, places, designs, versions, publication types, and outcomes where needed. Define the unit of analysis, such as study, incident, dataset, or release. Decide how to handle preprints, duplicate reports, missing information, and inaccessible material.
- Choose databases, registries, archives, and supplementary sources based on their coverage. For clinical questions, registries can expose completed unpublished or ongoing studies. For regulatory or technical topics, include the pertinent official docket or release history. Record access and coverage limits.
- Agree on extraction fields, risk-of-bias approach, comparison groups, and planned synthesis. If the protocol changes after seeing results, record when and why. For formal reviews, arrange independent screening, adjudication, or strategy review as required by the applicable method. Report single-reviewer decisions accurately.

## Run a reproducible search

Record each source and platform, date run, exact strategy as executed, filters, coverage period, and number retrieved. Save identifiers and record enough information to reproduce searches. When rankings affect a web search, record the number or depth screened. Log citation chaining and manual discovery separately. Verify translations across databases and test whether known eligible records can be found. If the decision depends on freshness, rerun critical searches near delivery.

Minimal search log

| Date and platform | Exact query and filters | Retrieved or screened | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| YYYY-MM-DD, database and interface | Full query as run | Count, or pages screened | Coverage, known-item check, limits, or deviation |

## Screen and extract

Deduplicate by stable identifiers and normalized metadata. Link multiple reports about the same underlying study or event, keeping distinct outcomes or follow-ups visible. Screen title and abstract or record summary first, then review the full evidence against the criteria. Record full-text exclusion reasons and separate access failures from exclusions. If decisions are uncertain, retain the record for adjudication.

Extract source locators, design, sample, conditions, outcomes, estimates and uncertainty, limitations, funding, and risk-of-bias judgments where relevant. Mark missing values as missing. Verify critical numbers against tables, appendices, and denominators. Carry through the outcome definition and time point.

## Synthesize and report

Group comparable evidence by question, population, intervention or exposure, outcome, version, or method. Evaluate consistency, directness, precision, missing evidence, and potential bias for each important finding. Weigh independent evidence chains. Avoid tallying statistically significant results as votes. Pool estimates only when the data and design support the comparison and the analyst can justify the model. Otherwise present a structured narrative or table with effects, uncertainty, and reasons results differ.

Report what was found, where and when you searched, counts where applicable, important exclusions, protocol deviations, and what the evidence permits you to conclude. Make missing or indirect evidence visible. PRISMA and its extensions guide reporting. Choose conduct and appraisal methods separately.
